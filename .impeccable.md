## Design Context

### Users
Aura is used by the homeowner and household members in a mixed-device environment: Sonoff NSPanel 120PW and NSPanel Pro Gen2 wall panels for glanceable control, iPhone and Fire HD for touch-first room control, iPad Pro for richer tablet workflows, and laptops for full home and audio management. The main jobs are checking the home at a glance, controlling lights and media quickly, understanding current Sonos sources, and issuing natural-language commands.

### Brand Personality
Aura should feel intelligent, tactile, and precise. It should communicate confidence through clear state, immediate feedback, and controls that feel physical and deliberate.

### Aesthetic Direction
Keep the existing Aura observatory-inspired dark interface and amber/cyan state language, but refine it into a calmer, more legible product surface. Avoid generic dashboard density, decorative clutter, and desktop layouts that merely shrink on touch devices. The same information architecture should adapt by context: glance-first on wall panels, thumb-first on phones, balanced on tablets, and information-rich on laptops.

### Design Principles
- Make live state obvious before offering secondary controls.
- Treat touch as a first-class input with generous, stable targets and no hover dependency.
- Use progressive disclosure so small screens stay focused without removing core actions.
- Preserve one coherent information architecture across wall panels, mobile, tablet, and desktop.
- Prefer precise status language and direct feedback over decorative UI.
